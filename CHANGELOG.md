# Changelog

All notable changes to the JFLib library -- the crates under `lib/`, the styles, the build
(`build_rs.py`) and its tools -- are recorded here. The format follows
[Keep a Changelog 1.1.0](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions follow
[Semantic Versioning 2.0.0](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html) as
[docs/VERSIONING.md](docs/VERSIONING.md) describes (what is public, deprecations, how
`build_rs.py api-diff` checks a release). Apps keep their own versions (`version=` in
`apps/NAME/PACKAGE`) and their own notes.

Every crate also has a `CHANGELOG.md` of its own in its folder, written from the crate's side, and
a `README.md` whose section "What's new" sums up its latest release ([docs/VERSIONING.md](docs/VERSIONING.md),
"Each crate's README and changelog").

Add entries with `python build_rs.py changelog "What changed" --type added|changed|deprecated|removed|fixed|security --area AREA`
(`--crate C,..`: in those crates' changelogs too); `python build_rs.py release X.Y.Z` turns
`[Unreleased]` into a release here and in every crate's changelog.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **build:** `build_rs.py test` runs the scripts side by side: `--test-jobs N` at a time (default half the logical CPUs, `jfgui_TEST_JOBS`; 1 = one after the other), Windows and Linux together, the checks after the scripts beside them. An app's scripts keep their order and never run on both targets at once; `#!lock desktop` / `#!lock audio` and `#!serial WHY` among a script's first lines, `apps/NAME/tests/jobs.txt` for a whole app (`independent`, `independent targets`, `lock`, `serial`); each job starts its Linux programs through one WSL process of its own (`linux_task.py serve`); render3dtest's and gputest's validation in parts (`--test A,B`, `--except`). At the end the times of build, scripts and checks and the slowest scripts and apps; `build/test-report.json` and `build/test-times.json` (the longest work first in the next run). `test --quick` with a warm build: 16 to 20 minutes under the load of other sessions instead of about 70 (63 of them scripts one after the other)
- **plat:** `vfs::Provider::create_new` (a new file where nothing is, the check and the creation in one step; the default uses the OS exclusive create of `os_path`) and `vfs::Stream::deferred` (bytes reach the provider only by sync or drop)
- **plat:** `fill_random`, `random_bytes` and `random_u64` on Android (bionic's `arc4random_buf`); the browser has none, which the docs now say (a web page takes `crypto.getRandomValues`)

- **ui:** A drop's answer to its source after the drop, for a target that learns later whether it took it (a web page's drop handler in another process): `ui.drop_defer()` in the pass of the drop (`DS_DROP`, `UIC_DROP`) keeps the answer, `ui.drop_finish(effect)` gives it (the effect, 0 = not taken; an effect the source does not allow becomes the first it does), at most `DROP_DEFER_MS` (3 s) later, else as the drop was delivered with a UIWARN; a drop that is not deferred is answered after its passes with the effect it was delivered with, as before. X11 sends `XdndFinished` (accepted, the action) then, Wayland finishes the offer or destroys it without finish, Windows ends the operation of a source in `IDataObjectAsyncCapability`'s async mode (`EndOperation`); macOS, the browser and other Windows sources answer at the drop (`drop_defer` says false). docs/GUIDE.md section 7 has what the source sees per platform
- **ui:** `ui.drag_allowed()`: the `DE_*` effects the source of the drag over the window allows (Windows the effects of its `DoDragDrop`, X11 its `XdndActionList`, Wayland its `source_actions`, macOS the operation mask, the app's own drag `DragData::allowed()`; `DE_ALL` where the platform does not say); after a drop the effects its source allows (as the platform gives them with the drop, else as the hover before it said), from its `E_DROP` to the end of its passes (`DS_DROP`, `UIC_DROP`) and, after `drop_defer`, until its answer went -- the effects `drop_finish` answers within; a target that hands them on with the drop need not remember the hover's
- **plat:** `drop_allowed()` (read after `drop_data_take`) and `drop_set_allowed(effects)` (the OS layers): the effects the source of a drop allows, as the platform gives them with the drop -- Windows `IDropTarget::Drop`'s effects, X11 the `XdndActionList` with the last `XdndPosition`'s action, Wayland the offer's `source_actions`, macOS the operation mask of `performDragOperation:`; 0 in the browser and on Android
- **plat:** `drop_hold(on)`, `drop_waiting()`, `drop_finish(serial, effect)`: the OS layers keep a drop's answer to its source open after `E_DROP` until the program gives it (the UI turns it on for its windows); Windows' data object of the app's drags offers `IDataObjectAsyncCapability` (async mode off); `dnd_test_drop_async` / `dnd_test_async_end` for plattest
- **ui:** Script commands `drop expect copy|move|link|none|pending` (the answer the last drop's source got) and `drop ... sync` (a platform that answers at the drop); `drop ... allows copy|move|link` (the effects the source allows, given with the drop); `dragover X Y ... copy|move|link` (the effects the source allows) is documented
- **Tests:** dndtest's "Später" defers its drops (tests/later.uis, also `drag_allowed` in a drop's pass and while its answer waits, a drop's own effects, a drag that left); tests/effects.uis drops refused for want of an effect the source allows; plattest --test dnd checks the answer after the drop through OLE with a data object in async mode and `drop_allowed`, --test dnd-x11 with `tools/platshared/dnd_x11.py` the held `XdndFinished`, `XdndActionList` and the drop's effects; viewtest has two app windows with their own title bars (tests/titlebars.uis) and a paragraph of 516 bytes (tests/longtext.uis)

### Changed

- **plat:** `File::create_new` on a mount whose provider has neither `Provider::create_new` nor `os_path` returns None (it was exclusive only against other `create_new` calls of the process; `write_file` or `File::open` of another writer in between was emptied). `log_file` returns false for a file of such a provider (its lines reached the provider only when the log stopped, a crash line never)
- **plat:** X11: the effects another program's drag allows are the source's `XdndActionList` with the action it asks for, in `E_DRAG` and for the `XdndStatus` answer (without a list every effect, as before)
- **cas:** `Repo::save_snapshot` and `Unsaved::save_snapshot` refuse a key the repository does not trust with the new `CasError::UntrustedSigner`. They added the key to the trusted signers by themselves, so `load_snapshot`'s signature check never refused a snapshot saved through the API and `trust_signer` changed nothing. Trusted are `CreateOptions::signer` and the keys given to `trust_signer`; a program that saves with another key calls `trust_signer` first. The list is protected by the repository key: it keeps out other keys and mistakes, not someone who can open the repository
- **plat:** `net::UnixListener::bind` removes only a socket file nobody listens on any more; a file, a folder or a socket that is listened on stays and `bind` fails (AddrInUse; a folder on Windows: Other). It removed whatever was at the path first: a file with its bytes, an empty folder, another listener's socket
- **base:** `base::Ini::load_checked` gives None when it cannot tell that the name is free (refused, an I/O error, a folder there) or cannot learn the file's size; only a missing name gives an empty Ini. `file_exists_checked` on a folder mount answers for its OS file (it gave the mount's kind: 0 for an error too)

### Fixed

- **ui:** The effects another program's drag allows are forgotten when it leaves the window, is dropped or the app's own drag ends, and a drop takes those the platform gives with it: a drop without a hover before it had the effects of the drag before it (or copy and move), for its effect and the answer `drop_finish` gives
- **ui:** A drop has an effect its source allows: when the platform's effect is not one the target takes (a scripted drop, one that raced the pass), it gets the first of the target's effects that the source allows -- it got the target's first effect, so a copy-only drop onto a move target was delivered as a move (`drop_effect()`) and answered as a copy later; with none in common the drop is refused (no `DS_DROP`, no `UIC_DROP`, the source is answered "not taken"), as the platform refuses such a drag over the target; outside the targets likewise
- **ui:** State slots are per window (`ui.slot`, the boxes' measurements, views' scroll positions, animations): the same explicit id in two windows -- the title bar's box, a dialog's box of a fixed id -- flipped between the windows' sizes in every pass ("layout did not settle within 4 passes"); a window's slots go with its state, a panel floating out into a tool window starts there with fresh ones
- **ui:** Script selectors and `expect S text` compare a record's whole label: a quoted string was cut at 255 bytes, so a wrapped paragraph longer than that never matched (script lines hold up to 16 KiB now); `dump` writes labels up to 64 KiB (it cut them at 172 bytes, also inside a character)
- **wgsl:** A compound assignment of a vector with a scalar (`v += 1.0`, `v -= s`, `v /= 2.0`, `w += 1` of an integer vector, also through a swizzle) splats the scalar as `v + 1.0` does: in SPIR-V `v += 1.0` became a multiply by 1.0 (`-=` and `/=` alike) and integer vectors gave invalid SPIR-V; a float `v *= s` stays a vector times scalar
- **fx:** black_white_plus with its default "Leuchtdichte" divides the weighted sum by 1000 (it divided by 100, so nearly every pixel came out white); efftest's self test checks its answer
- **img_jxl:** The edge-preserving filter (EPF) of a frame with chroma subsampling reads each colour plane with its own stride: after upsampling, the chroma planes' stride differs from luma's when the width is no multiple of 16, and the filter read their rows from the wrong place (valid files decoded with wrong colours, up to 144 levels off); codectest --jxlref compares such files with libjxl 0.7 (tools/jxl_epf_fixtures.py)
- **doc_io:** HTML clipboard round trips keep the size of raised and lowered text: the HTML holds the shown script size in font-size and the import took it for the nominal size, so every copy and paste shrank such text by a third; the nominal size travels in data-jf-size
- **doc_io:** HTML clipboard round trips keep the line spacing: the import cut the decimals of line-height (250/240 lines, written 1.041666, came back as 249) and rounds them now
- **gpu:** Range checks add without wrapping: push_constants, dispatch_indirect, texture_barrier_range, create_view, the region and data span of texture copies, bind group and bindless buffer ranges (Vulkan and Metal), and the browser's copy, clear, indirect, immediate and view checks: an offset or base near u32::MAX / u64::MAX wrapped the sum small, passed the check and reached the driver; gputest checks push constants and views at u32::MAX
- **shader:** An instruction longer than SPIR-V's 65535 words is written with the word count 0 (never valid) instead of a count that wrapped into the next instructions; wgsl reports it ("an instruction is longer than SPIR-V's 65535 words": a name of 262,140 characters or more, a constant with that many components)
- **plat:** Ogg Vorbis floor 1 keeps its values in 15 bits as libvorbis does: a codebook entry up to 2^24 overflowed i32 in the next point's interpolation (a wrong curve, with overflow checks an abort); valid streams decode as before
- **spell:** COMPOUNDMIN is clamped to 1..=65535: 2^63 wrapped to 0 when doubled and the compound search indexed past the word (an abort when checking a word)
- **gfx:** ellipse_fx and rrect_fx compute their control offsets in 64 bits: from a radius of about 59,500 px (rounded rectangles: 73,000 px) they wrapped and the shape came out wrong
- **ui:** The syntax highlighter takes a block whose depth was lost in the 32-bit line state (shifted out below three nested constructs) as one open block, so `*/` closes it (the depth went below 0 and the rest of the line stayed a comment); a line state without a rule byte is no construct
- **img_tga, img_webp, img_bmp, img_jxl, netpkt:** Offsets and sizes from a file are checked without wrapping a 32-bit usize (wasm32): TGA's extension offset (a read past the data), WebP chunk sizes near 4 GB (the walk went back into the file), a BMP's ICC profile offset, JXL plane sizes (ERR_NOMEM instead of a plane too small), pcapng block and packet lengths (an endless loop, a bad slice)
- **img_jxl:** The spline area estimate saturates instead of wrapping: a huge quantizer adjustment or colour factor wrapped it small and let the area limit pass
- Overflows that only builds with overflow checks saw (a debug build of an app without JFLib's profile aborted): img_heif's encoder for one image without metadata, md_doc's update of changed blocks, the terminal's resize anchor after the scrollback dropped lines, X11's work area, a FILETIME from 2^63 on (Windows), the underline position of variable fonts, shaped glyph offsets
- **plat:** `File::create_new` and `AtomicFile`'s sibling on mounts check and create in one step (`Provider::create_new`: `MemFs`, `Overlay`, `Sub`, content://; folder mounts the OS exclusive create): another writer's file made between the check and the open was emptied by `create_new` and taken over by `AtomicFile`, which then removed it on drop or renamed it at commit (also on `DirFs` mounts)
- **plat:** content:// (`ContentFs`): a folder move to another folder is refused before the file in its way is deleted (it was deleted, then the move refused); an open for writing or a rename the provider refuses keeps the pending journal copy for `recover` (it was dropped before asking); writes of one document with a journal run one after the other (also `recover` and the removal of a copy), so a write that succeeded no longer removed the copy of a later one that failed
- **plat:** Linux trash on a file system without `RENAME_NOREPLACE`: the fallback looked whether the name in `Trash/files` was free and then renamed, replacing an entry another program made in between; now a file goes by a hard link and the removal of its old name, a folder replaces an empty folder made for it (steps that fail on a taken name); a file system without hard links refuses files
- **plat:** Windows secrets: two writes of one large entry at once (or a small and a large one) could leave the credential pointing at a DPAPI file the other write had swept; set, get and delete of an entry hold `data://secrets/<hash>.lock` (an exclusive handle, across processes) from the file to the sweep
- **doc_io:** A source list that `encode_sources` / `save_sources` writes loads back: `decode_sources` / `load_sources` read with the same limits, 8 MiB and 250000 elements (they stopped at 4 MiB and 30000, so a long list could be saved but not opened); `decode_bibliography` / `load_bibliography` take Word XML up to 8 MiB too
- **img_avif:** The AVIF writer takes quality 0 as 95, like `io::opt_init(IMGF_AVIF)` and the other writers (it was 85)
- **text:** macOS and iOS: the system fallback and `FontConf`'s built-in tables name Apple's fonts (Helvetica Neue, Hiragino, Heiti, Apple SD Gothic Neo, Thonburi, Kohinoor, Geeza Pro, ...); they named Linux families, so Chinese, Japanese, Korean and other scripts found no font there. Emoji still have none: Apple Color Emoji's glyphs are sbix bitmaps, which `text` does not draw
- **img_webp:** No dependency on `plat` any more (it used nothing of it); `IOF_WEBP_1THREAD` and `WEF_1THREAD` are documented as without effect, the encoder has no worker threads
- **netio:** `Client::new()` sends the user agent `JFLib-netio` and `ssh::Client` the version `SSH-2.0-JFLib_netio` (both still named CraftLIB); a mail without a HELO name gets its Message-ID at `jflib` instead of `backupcraft`
- **gpu:** `GpuError::NoLoader` never comes on macOS and iOS (no Metal device is `NoAdapter`): the docs say so, and its text there no longer claims a missing Metal device
- **build:** The manifests' docs.rs comments (and `tools/scaffold.py`'s) no longer call macOS none of JFLib's targets
- **netio:** `smtp::send` with `Security::None`: the docs (GUIDE section 17, the README) say what the code does, a plain connection to any server (a relay on port 25 inside a network) and no login without TLS; they said "plain on loopback"
- **plat:** Windows logout and shutdown: `WM_QUERYENDSESSION` ended the app already (`UIC_SESSION_END`, then `UIC_EXIT`), also when another app cancelled the logout; and the app saved only after `WM_ENDSESSION` had returned, when Windows may end the process. Now only a confirmed `WM_ENDSESSION` sends it, and the UI takes it while the message is handled (when it comes during the wait for events; elsewhere it waits in the queue)
- **plat:** An OS path with a NUL character reached the system cut at the NUL: `delete`, `File::open` for writing, `rename`, `fs::write_file_private` and the rest changed the file named by the part before it. `policy::fs_allowed` refuses such a path, also without a manifest, so every file call fails; `fs::symlink` refuses such a target
- **plat:** Windows: `File::write_at` at offset u64::MAX appended to the file (the OVERLAPPED value for "at the end") and reported success; an offset whose end passes i64::MAX is refused before anything is written, for `read_at` too
- **plat:** The log file counted a short write as the whole line: the rest was lost, the next line started past the end (a gap of NUL bytes), the rotation came early. Only the bytes written count now, and the rest of the line follows
- **plat:** content:// (`ContentFs`): a new document the provider named `name (1)` (the name taken meanwhile) took the bytes of `write_file` or a writing `File::open`, which reported success; now that document goes again and the one under the name asked for is written (`mkdir` takes the folder made meanwhile instead of failing and leaving `name (1)`). The chosen folder could be deleted by its document form (`tree/ID/document/ID`)
- **plat:** `vfs::Sub`: opening its root for writing emptied or created a file of the folder's name in the inner provider and was refused only then; refused first now (`create_new` too)
- **plat:** `aio::Writer`: a write whose future was dropped while its job ran could overwrite a later write at the same offset, and the commit succeeded. Such a writer is spoilt now: later writes give `Err(Write)`, the commit `Err(Commit)`, nothing is put in place
- **base:** `Ini::load_checked` returned an empty Ini for a file it could not open when asking whether it exists failed (refused, an I/O error), and for a file whose size was unknown: a later save replaced the settings it never read

### Fixed

- **build:** `build_rs.py release` writes cargo-jflib's version into Cargo.lock as well (before, `cargo --locked` failed after a release)
- **JFNetworker:** A device with many TCP connections, TIME-WAIT among them, spent its time looking for the next deadline on every segment; a load generator with thousands of requests in flight looked through its connections for each one. Both find them at once now: such runs take a seventieth to a five-hundredth of the time, with the same reports.
- **rt:** Small blocks of the allocator live in 64 heaps with a lock each, chosen by the stack's address, instead of one lock for all: threads that allocate all the time no longer wait for each other (a simulation's sweep took as long on 4 threads as on 1, and now under half).
- **JFNetworker:** A closed-loop load generator no longer stops for good when its target goes away and requests fail as they are made (no route, ARP gave up): it tries again every 100 ms
- **JFNetworker:** A script service with http.serve no longer stops after about 11 000 requests: what the service makes for a callback is collected also when the callback allocates nothing itself, and a request the handler cannot start for (heap full, frame too large) is answered 500 instead of staying open

### Security

- **plat:** Wayland: the sizes of the shared-memory buffers of windows and popups are computed without 32-bit wrap-around; a frame whose buffers would pass the protocol's 2 GiB pool limit stays unshown instead of being copied past the mapping
